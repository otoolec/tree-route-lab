When are all the trees in?

Trailers leave Cedar Park at 8:00. Each free trailer takes the longest load left, drives it from wherever it is, and unloads (10 min) at Cedar Park before noon or Robin Bledsoe Park after. Every trailer takes one 30-minute lunch. Pick the minutes per stop; nobody has measured it yet.

Two planner questions, answered

Can sheets be printed Friday night?

Yes, if late sign-ups stay near 20. Taking 20 random stops out, planning the rest, then slotting the 20 into the cheapest load with room cost about 1.5% more trailer time on average (worst of 20 tries: 3.7%). With 40 late stops it was 4.5 to 5.4% on average and up to 11%, so past about 20 late stops it pays to re-plan.

Is a full drive-time table needed?

A partial one is enough. Keeping road times for each stop's 30 nearest neighbours (about 15,000 lookups, 11% of the full table) planned within 0.3 to 2.4% of the full table. Planning on straight-line distance alone kept total time close but produced 128 to 133 minute loads, so the 90-minute cap stopped meaning anything.

What a driver could get

Today a driver gets a sheet whose stops are in sign-up order and one Google Maps link with every address in it. Google Maps keeps that order and won't reorder it, and on a phone it takes only about 10 stops, so a 20-stop link gets cut short.

The mock on the left is one load from the two-base plan, laid out the way a driver would use it: stops numbered in driving order, the map link split into legs of up to 9 stops, and a tap per stop for "picked up", "no tree" or "no money". The taps also record the time of each stop, which is the one number every result on this page is waiting for.

Recommendations after round 3

For the day itself

  1. Number the stops in driving order. Works with the routes the troop already draws, and straight-line distances are enough for it.small
  2. Hand out the longest loads first. With the published routes and 8 trailers, longest-first finished about an hour sooner than nearest-first at every stop time tested. It costs nothing.changed
  3. Record stop times in January 2027. Every finish time here moves by an hour or more between 2 and 4 minutes per stop. The phone checklist, or a start and end time written on each sheet, settles it.small
  4. Count trailers before changing the planner. At 3 minutes per stop the troop needs 7 trailers to finish by 3 pm with any good plan, and 7 with ordered published routes too.small

For the planning code

  1. Use the two-base savings plan instead of K-means: stops split by which drop-off is nearer by road, then Clarke-Wright savings with a route-shape setting of 1.6 and a 90-minute cap. Plain Python, a tenth of a second.changed
  2. Skip OR-Tools and skip zones. The shape setting gives tighter routes than K-means zones for less time, and the solver's extra trees per load don't finish the day any sooner.changed
  3. Split big orders automatically into trailer-sized pieces before planning, as before.small
  4. Insert late sign-ups into the cheapest load with room, and re-plan only past about 20 of them.small
  5. If staying on Google for drive times, fetch only each stop's 30 nearest neighbours plus the two drop-offs.medium

How this was tested

  • Stops, tree counts and route assignments come from the public 2026 and 2025 route maps on troop202.site. Drive times are from OSRM over OpenStreetMap roads, so they leave out traffic and slow trailer turns.
  • Plan metrics count each load from and back to its base. The day simulation re-orders every load for where the trailer really starts and which drop-off is open when it returns.
  • Finish times within about 15 minutes of each other are a tie: moving one load to a different trailer shifts them that much.
  • The capacity is one 22-tree limit for every trailer. The real fleet mix and tree heights are not modeled yet.
  • Nothing was changed in the troop's repository. Scripts and results are in the project's tree-routes/round3 folder.
